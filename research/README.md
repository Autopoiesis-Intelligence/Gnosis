# GNOSIS Research

## Research and Core versioning

This directory contains experimental research conducted with GNOSIS and
the Gnosis core implementation.

The purpose of this directory is to separate experimental investigation
from the stable core implementation.

---

## Research Areas

Research may include:

* artificial intelligence;
* autonomous systems;
* self-organizing systems;
* autopoietic systems;
* recursive computation;
* scientific modeling;
* complex systems;
* machine reasoning;
* model evolution;
* optimization;
* simulation;
* mathematical analysis;
* computational experiments;
* interdisciplinary research.

---

## Research Status

Research contained in this directory may represent different levels of
maturity.

Each investigation should distinguish between:

**Hypothesis** — a proposed explanation or mechanism.

**Experiment** — a computational or analytical test.

**Result** — an observed outcome of an experiment.

**Validation** — independent confirmation of a result.

**Established finding** — a result supported by sufficient evidence.

GNOSIS does not automatically treat experimental output as a validated
scientific discovery.

---

## Reproducibility

Research experiments should provide enough information for independent
investigation whenever practical.

Where applicable, record:

* GNOSIS version;
* Core implementation version;
* Ψ-Core version;
* Git commit;
* experiment configuration;
* parameters;
* input data;
* output data;
* software dependencies;
* hardware environment;
* random seeds;
* evaluation method.

---

## Experimental Structure

A research project may use a structure such as:

```text
research/
└── experiment-name/
    ├── README.md
    ├── experiment.py
    ├── results/
    └── data/
```

Large datasets should not be committed to the repository unnecessarily.

---

## Independent Research

Researchers are encouraged to conduct independent experiments,
including experiments that contradict the assumptions or expected
behavior of GNOSIS.

Negative results, failed experiments and counterexamples are valuable
research contributions.

---

## Scientific Integrity

Research should clearly distinguish:

```text
Observation
    ↓
Interpretation
    ↓
Hypothesis
    ↓
Test
    ↓
Result
    ↓
Independent Validation
```

No computational result should be presented as established science
without appropriate evidence.
